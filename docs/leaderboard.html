Observation — the two 3B-class models are refuted most often in this snapshot. Refuted share of proposed rewrites: 45.9% (Qwen2.5-3B) and 49.1% (Llama-3.2-3B). The other four models span 25.0–40.0%: Qwen2.5-7B 25.0%, Phi-3.5 3.8B 26.7%, DeepSeek-Coder-V2 35.1%, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B 40.0%. Size is not destiny — Phi-3.5 3.8B sits inside that range — but the two 3B models are refuted at nearly half of their proposals and also carry most execution failures: Qwen2.5-3B 16 errors + 6 unsupported, Llama-3.2-3B 10 errors + 1 unsupported, Phi-3.5 15 unsupported + 3 errors. Together the three smallest models account for 51 of the 56 invalid outcomes (errors + unsupported) in the dataset.

What a refutation looks like

Four of the six models were refuted on this family — three collapsed UNION ALL into OR, DeepSeek added DISTINCT over the union; either rewrite loses duplicates. One frozen state exposes it; the dataset stores the state and both result sets, and the refuting pair was replayed in a fresh schema.

How to read these numbers

Outcomes are about evidence, not about “model quality” in the abstract

✕Refuted

At least one frozen database state makes the candidate return a different result than the reference query. The state, both result sets and a replay flag are stored in data/evidence/.

One counterexample is enough: a rewrite is wrong if any allowed state breaks it.

✓Preserved

The candidate differs from the reference SQL, and no state in the finite bank broke it. This is not a proof of equivalence — the bank is finite and cannot cover all possible databases.

Read it as “no evidence against on this bank”, never as “correct rewrite”.

–No rewrite

The model returned the reference query unchanged. That is neither a success nor a failure: there was no rewrite to validate.

Unsupported candidates and execution errors are separate outcomes and are reported explicitly, never folded into “no rewrite”.

ΣWhy checks beat shares

A pass rate hides the counterexample: one broken state among 5,712 checks is still a wrong rewrite. That is why this table shows refuting checks (629, 444, 415, 141, 111, 81) over executed checks (5,712), not percentages alone. Refuted share is shown for scale; the counterexample count is the evidence.

Every check is one executable state; the count is directly auditable in the data.
